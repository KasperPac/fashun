# Test script: KAS-628 "Style me: let off-palette pieces into outfits"

Written by QA (KAS-629) from the KAS-628 outcome, its 8 acceptance checks and the Delivery Manager's decisions A1–A3 (25 Nov 2026). No Engineer branch was read, and nothing here says how to build it. Base: `origin/master` at `4ca7b8f`. In the repository this file goes in `docs/test-scripts/KAS-628.md`.

**Markers**
- `automated`: the Engineer writes a test with the given name, and it runs with the repository's tests. The model is replaced by a stand-in that returns fixed suggestions. No automated test calls the real model.
- `real-use`: QA runs it on the QA preview (`agent/qa-fashun`, at the reviewed commit) through the real screens, as QA accounts A and B only, with a desktop (1920 × 1080) and phone (390 × 844) screenshot per step.
- `live-safe`: safe on the live site (`fashun-web.vercel.app`) as the QA accounts only. Each Style me run is one model call (a fraction of a US cent); nothing else costs money.
- `baseline`: run on the live site **before** the merge, while it still runs `master` (check 7's "today"). QA first proves live runs `4ca7b8f` (`deployments?sha=…&environment=Production`); if live has moved on, QA records the commit it runs and the Delivery Manager decides whether it still counts as "today".

The preview runs on the live database, so the QA accounts' wardrobes are the same on preview and live. That's what lets the baseline (live, today's code) and the real-use run (preview, the branch) use the **same wardrobe**.

**Words used below**
- **Off-palette piece** (A1): a piece where **none** of its colours passes the season rule, or any of its colours is one of the account's saved avoid colours. On the wardrobe screen this is the piece whose sheet says "Outside your Autumn palette. It can still be used in outfits."
- **In-palette colour** (A1): a single colour that passes the season rule **and** isn't a saved avoid colour. Only these may be described as in the palette.
- **Palette claim**: wording that puts a colour (or the whole outfit) inside the palette: "… is/are (both) in your {Season} palette", "… one of your best {Season} colours", "built from your {Season} palette".
- **False claim**: a palette claim about a colour that isn't an in-palette colour (for example "Blue and camel are both in your Autumn palette"). Any false claim on screen fails checks 3 and 5.
- **Today's wording** (check 4): "{Colour A} and {colour B} are both in your {Season} palette, and …" or "{Colour A} is one of your best {Season} colours, and …".
- **Run**: tapping Casual (or another occasion) on Style me once and waiting for the result.
- **Reason**: the line under an outfit that says why it works.

**Reason rules every real-use step checks** (check 6): one sentence (no `. `, `! ` or `? ` followed by more text), at most 140 characters, colour names only (no `#` followed by six hex digits). QA copies each reason verbatim into the evidence and counts its characters.

## Interpretations QA applies (the Delivery Manager can overrule)

1. **Check 2 is judged on what the model returns.** A2 makes "at most one" prompt guidance, so a model outfit with two off-palette pieces is kept by the server; QA still records it as a failed R6 because check 2 says "an outfit has at most one". QA reports how many of the outfits broke it.
2. **Check 3 and the fallback.** When the model's reason is rejected, the fallback only has to be true (check 5). It needn't name the off-palette colour and what balances it. A **model** reason for a shirt outfit that ignores the shirt is recorded under R2 as a check 3 failure.
3. **Saved avoid colours** can't be edited through the app today, so the "in the palette but a saved avoid colour" case (A1) is covered by T9 and T10 only.
